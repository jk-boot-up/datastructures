1. Circular Linked List (Recursive)

Hello, and welcome. This video explains the circular linked list, written recursively, in Java. This video is presented by Jayasekhar Konduru. First, a simple definition. A circular linked list has no null: the last node points back to the first. So a recursion over it must stop somewhere else: at the last node, the end of one lap. Our example is players taking turns round a board game. By the end, you will know how to choose that base case, and a recursive formula for the counting-out game.

2. The Everyday Idea

Picture the players round the table. The dealer asks the player on the left: how many from you round to me? Each player asks the next, and waits. The player just before the dealer answers: one, just me. That is the base case. If everyone waited for a player with nobody to their left, the question would go round for ever.

3. The Words

The words. A lap goes from the first node round to the last. The base case is the last node, never null. Nodes are found recursively and linked in one step. And the Josephus formula finds the counting-out winner.

4. Act One — The base case is last, not null

First, the base case. Count of Ann is one, plus count of Ben. Count of Ben is one, plus count of Cat. And so on, to Dan. Dan is last, the end of the lap. So count of Dan is just one. In a circle there is no null. A recursion waiting for null would go round for ever.

10. Act Four — Counting out, by a recursive formula

The counting-out game has a recursive formula. With one player, the winner is at position zero. With n players, the winner is the winner for n minus one, moved on k places, round the circle. For five players, counting in threes, the formula says position three: Dan. The same winner, without building the circle.

12. Act Five — The limit of recursion

And the limit. A million players join with no recursion. Counting them recursively needs a million frames, and the stack overflows. The loop version goes round with order one extra space.

13. The limit of recursion

One call per player. Far too many.

14. Time and Space Complexity

Here are the costs. Both ends are order one, with no recursion. Display, count and search take order n time and stack. So does deleting at the end, and the Josephus formula. The loop versions take the same time, with order one space.

15. Recursive or Loop?

Recursive or loop? The recursion stops at the last node; the loop is a do while that comes back to the start. The time is the same. On a million nodes, only the loop finishes.

16. Common Mistakes

Four mistakes. A base case waiting for null. Starting the lap at the last node instead of the first. Recursing to reach an end, which the last pointer gives for free. And recursing round long circles.
