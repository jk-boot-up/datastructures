1. Doubly Linked List (Recursive)

Hello, and welcome. This video explains the doubly linked list, written recursively, in Java. This video is presented by Jayasekhar Konduru. First, a simple definition. A doubly linked list has a previous and a next pointer in every node. Here, every walk is a recursion, which can follow either pointer. Finding a node is recursive; changing the links is still one step. Our example is a photo viewer. By the end, you will know how recursion walks a list in both directions, and what it costs.

2. The Everyday Idea

Think of guards on a train. Asked how many carriages are behind, a guard asks the guard in the next carriage back, and waits. At the last carriage, the answer is none. Then the answers come back, each guard adding one. The same works towards the front. That is recursion on a doubly linked list.

3. The Words

The words. A recursion can follow the next pointer, or the previous pointer. The base case is null, or the node you want. Finding is recursive, and linking is one step. And the depth is one frame per node walked.

6. Act Two — Both ends need no recursion

Both ends need no recursion. Inserting at the beginning and at the end uses the head and the tail directly. Depth zero. So does deleting at the end. Counting must visit every node, so it recurses six deep.

Reversal, recursively. Each call swaps its node's previous and next pointers. Then it recurses on the old next, which it saved first. Five calls deep. Finally the head and the tail are swapped.

12. Act Five — The limit of recursion

And the limit. A million photos, inserted at the end, with no recursion. Counting them recursively needs a million frames. The stack overflows. The loop version needs order one extra space.

13. The limit of recursion

One call per node. Far too many.

14. Time and Space Complexity

Here are the costs. Insertion and deletion at both ends are order one, with no recursion. Display, count and search take order n time and stack. In the middle, finding is order n, and linking order one. Reverse is order n time and stack. The loop version takes the same time, with order one space.

15. Recursive or Loop?

Recursive or loop? The time is the same. Recursion adds a frame per node walked. At both ends, neither needs a walk. On a million nodes, only the loop finishes.

16. Common Mistakes

Four mistakes. In reverse, recursing on next after the swap. Recursing to reach the tail, which the tail pointer gives for free. Not updating the head or tail when unlinking an end. And recursing over long lists.
